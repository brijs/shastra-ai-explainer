# Storyboard — "Why Shastras, in the Age of AI?" (working title)

An animated, narrated explainer of Dr. Pratosh's talk. Style: palm-leaf manuscript. Guide: a gentle Acharya. Narration: Microsoft `en-IN-PrabhatNeural`. Verses read aloud: `hi-IN-MadhurNeural` (from Devanagari). 17 scenes. Every verse is decoded the same way: **Devanagari → padaccheda (word split) → word-by-word meaning → dhātu (root) → meaning of the whole → "why he quoted this"**.

Confidence badge on every verse: ● verified source, ◐ recognised but wording may differ, ○ reconstructed from audio, not matched to any source (decoded only as far as the words are clear).

## Decoding method (so it stays accurate)

- Each verse has: a data block (Devanagari, IAST, padaccheda, per-word gloss, dhātu + dhātu meaning, grammatical note, whole-verse meaning, source, confidence).
- I will use only roots and grammar I can state with confidence. Anything uncertain is shown as "likely" or omitted, not guessed.
- V2, V4, V5 and V11 could not be matched to a source online. The scene says so openly and decodes only the clear words.
- Verse audio is generated from the Devanagari with Madhur at a slower rate. Hindi voices read Sanskrit approximately, so sounds like *ṛ*, *ś*, *ṣ* and vowel length may be imperfect. The IAST and Devanagari stay on screen as the authority. I'll add a small note in the explainer.

## Narration

One entry per scene, ~35–60 spoken words, second person, in the Acharya's voice. Verses are read separately (not in the narrator's voice). Where English narration mentions a Sanskrit term, I'll add a pronunciation respelling so the narrator says it cleanly.

## Out of scope

- No political commentary. The talk thanks organisations and leaders on stage; the explainer keeps to the ideas and scriptures.
- No claim that the Shastras "contain" AI or modern science. The explainer sticks to the speaker's own caveat (scene 6).

## Grammar layer (added after review)
Every word in every verse card shows its type, case/number/gender (or person/tense/voice), stem or root, a use note, and the full table with the form highlighted. The Grammar guide (header) adds cases, number and gender, word types, notation, sounds and a 26-table paradigm gallery. Tables are generated and checked in `gen_grammar.py`.
